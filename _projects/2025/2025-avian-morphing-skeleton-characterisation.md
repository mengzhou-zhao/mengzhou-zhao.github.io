  <div class="mb-3 pl-3" style="border-left: 3px solid #ced4da;">
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>Problem</strong> &mdash; A morphing wing reshapes continuously, so its stiffness and strength change right across the range of motion &mdash; not something a single number can capture. To design a morphing skeleton that could actually fly, you first have to know how stiff it is at every extension, and where it breaks first.</p>
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>My role</strong> &mdash; I built the cantilever rig (fine feed stage + universal-joint loading), developed a backlash-separation routine, and ran the rigidity and failure tests.</p>
    <p class="mb-0"><strong>Impact</strong> &mdash; Found that specific stiffness has a minimum fixed by geometry (not material), ranked which bones govern overall rigidity, and pinpointed the humerus&ndash;bearing joint as the first interface to fail &mdash; findings that tell the next design exactly where to reinforce and where to spend material, getting a morphing wing's strength-to-weight right first time.</p>
  </div>

  An experimental campaign quantifying how the bird-inspired four-bar CFRP skeleton behaves across its full range of wing extension. Tests were run on a purpose-built cantilever rig &mdash; an aluminium-extrusion frame with a milling-machine-style feed stage for fine tip positioning, loaded through a universal-joint coupling so only a clean vertical force reaches the structure. Because the linkage and actuator carry mechanical clearance, a dedicated stiffness-extraction routine was developed to separate the true elastic response from backlash before fitting each configuration's stiffness. The headline result: specific stiffness passes through a consistent minimum at a particular span-extension configuration that is fixed by the four-bar linkage <em>geometry</em>, not by material or reinforcement &mdash; a finding corroborated analytically through a Jacobian / virtual-joint stiffness mapping. Reinforcement studies then established a clear hierarchy of which bones govern global rigidity, with the humerus dominant. Failure tests at the critical minimum-stiffness configuration showed that tailored multi-bone reinforcement markedly raises strength-to-weight, while failure consistently localises at the humerus&ndash;bearing joint &mdash; identifying that interface as the limiting structural feature for future redesign.

  一项实验研究，量化仿鸟类四连杆碳纤维复合材料骨架在机翼全展开范围内的力学行为。试验在一套专门搭建的悬臂台架上进行——一个铝型材框架配以铣床式进给台以实现端部的精细定位，并通过万向节联轴器加载，使得只有纯净的竖向力传递到结构上。由于连杆机构与作动器存在机械间隙，研究开发了专门的刚度提取算法，在拟合每种构型的刚度之前，将真实弹性响应与间隙回差分离开来。核心结论是：比刚度在某一特定的展开构型处稳定地经过一个极小值，而该构型由四连杆机构的<em>几何</em>决定，与材料或加强无关——这一发现通过雅可比 / 虚拟关节刚度映射得到了解析层面的印证。随后的加强研究明确了各“骨骼”对整体刚度的主次层级，其中肱骨起主导作用。在临界的最小刚度构型下进行的失效试验表明，有针对性的多骨加强能显著提升强重比，而失效始终局部化于肱骨&ndash;轴承关节处——这将该界面确定为未来再设计中的结构薄弱环节。

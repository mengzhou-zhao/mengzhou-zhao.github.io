summary: >-
  <div class="mb-3 pl-3" style="border-left: 3px solid #ced4da;">
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>Problem</strong> &mdash; A wing that folds, extends and continuously reshapes like a bird's could keep one aircraft efficient across cruise, manoeuvre and loiter alike &mdash; a direction for the next generation of adaptive aircraft. But such a wing needs a light, many-jointed skeleton, and discrete hinges and metal brackets both add mass and scatter assembly failure points throughout it.</p>
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>My role</strong> &mdash; I developed the end-to-end manufacturing process: continuous filament winding over water-soluble PVA jigs with the bearings co-cured directly into the joints.</p>
    <p class="mb-0"><strong>Impact</strong> &mdash; A monolithic CFRP four-bar skeleton with the joints co-cured directly in, no metal brackets at all, and stiffness precisely tunable per pass (mass R&sup2;&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.997) &mdash; a repeatable, predictable process that turns a joint-integrated lightweight morphing mechanism from a concept into something you can actually build.</p>
  </div>

  A continuous filament winding process that co-cures the truss "bones", revolute bearings and attachment nodes of a bird-inspired four-bar wing skeleton (humerus, radius, ulna, carpometacarpus) into a single monolithic CFRP part &mdash; with no discrete hinges, bonded connectors or metallic brackets. Each bone is wound from continuous 3k carbon tow (200&nbsp;tex, EL2 epoxy) directly onto a sacrificial jig FDM-printed in water-soluble PVA, with reusable translucent PETG inserts (bearing blocks, Y-shaped chord holders and threaded "tow catchers") that let fibre placement be inspected during winding. Oil-impregnated bronze bushings and threaded inserts are pre-positioned in the jig so they become co-cured directly into the joint regions; fibres pass continuously through each joint to form a locally reinforced hinge in situ. After a 24&nbsp;h ambient stand and a 60&nbsp;&deg;C / 6&nbsp;h oven cure, the PVA core is dissolved in 35&nbsp;&deg;C water (3&ndash;4&nbsp;h), leaving the finished skeleton; assembly into a working four-bar mechanism needs only M2 screws driven through each bearing into the co-cured tow catcher as the pivot axle. Member stiffness is tailored purely through winding count, and mass deposition scales linearly with pass count (R&sup2;&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.997), giving predictable, well-controlled reinforcement per pass.

  <div class="mb-3 pl-3" style="border-left: 3px solid #ced4da;">
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>问题</strong> &mdash; 像鸟一样折叠、展开、连续改变形态的变形机翼，能让同一架飞行器在巡航、机动与盘旋等不同状态下都保持高效——这正是下一代自适应飞行器的方向。但这样的机翼需要轻而多关节的骨架，而分立铰链与金属支架不仅增重，还处处埋下装配失效点。</p>
    <p class="mb-1"><strong>我的角色</strong> &mdash; 我打通了完整的制造流程：在水溶性 PVA 工装上连续纤维缠绕，并将轴承直接共固化在关节处。</p>
    <p class="mb-0"><strong>成果与应用</strong> &mdash; 得到一体式碳纤维四连杆骨架，关节直接共固化在内、全无金属支架，刚度还能按缠绕道数精确调节（质量 R&sup2;&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.997）——用一条可复制、可预测的制造路线，把"关节一体化的轻量变形机构"从概念变成了真正能做出来的东西。</p>
  </div>

  一种连续纤维缠绕工艺，将仿鸟类四连杆机翼骨架（肱骨、桡骨、尺骨、腕掌骨）的桁架“骨骼”、转动轴承与连接节点共固化为一个整体式碳纤维复合材料零件——无需任何分立铰链、粘接连接件或金属支架。每根“骨骼”都由连续 3k 碳纤维束（200&nbsp;tex，EL2 环氧）直接缠绕在以水溶性 PVA 通过 FDM 打印的牺牲性工装上，工装中嵌有可重复使用的半透明 PETG 插件（轴承块、Y 形弦杆夹持件和带螺纹的“捕束器”），使得缠绕过程中可对纤维铺放进行检查。含油青铜衬套与螺纹嵌件被预先安放在工装内，从而在关节区域直接被共固化在一起；纤维连续穿过每个关节，在原位形成局部加强的铰链。经过 24&nbsp;h 常温静置和 60&nbsp;&deg;C / 6&nbsp;h 烘箱固化后，将 PVA 芯在 35&nbsp;&deg;C 水中溶解（3&ndash;4&nbsp;h），即得到成品骨架；装配成可运动的四连杆机构只需用 M2 螺钉穿过每个轴承拧入共固化的捕束器，作为转动轴即可。杆件刚度完全通过缠绕道数来定制，质量沉积量随道数线性增加（R&sup2;&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.997），使每一道缠绕带来的加强都可预测、可精确控制。
